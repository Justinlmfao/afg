A look back, written in October 2026, at the stamp duty change in the 2025–26
Budget.

#### What changed

**A bigger HK$100 band.** Buying a home in Hong Kong means paying ad valorem
stamp duty on the price. For the cheapest homes it has been a flat HK$100. The
2025–26 Budget (26 February 2025) raised the top of that band from **HK$3
million to HK$4 million**, for property deals from that day. The Legislative
Council passed the law on 7 May 2025.

**How the duty works around the line.** Above the HK$100 band there's a short
"marginal relief" band, so the duty climbs steeply instead of jumping straight
to the full rate, and then a flat 1.5% up to HK$4.5 million.

- **Before:** HK$100 up to HK$3m; then HK$100 plus 10% of the amount over HK$3m,
  up to HK$3,528,240; then 1.5%.
- **After:** HK$100 up to HK$4m; then HK$100 plus 20% of the amount over HK$4m,
  up to HK$4,323,780; then 1.5%.

| Price | Duty before | Duty after | Saving |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HK$3.0m | HK$100 | HK$100 | None |
| HK$3.5m | HK$50,100 | HK$100 | HK$50,000 |
| HK$4.0m | HK$60,000 | HK$100 | HK$59,900 |
| HK$4.2m | HK$63,000 | HK$40,100 | HK$22,900 |
| HK$4.4m | HK$66,000 | HK$66,000 | None |

The saving peaks at **HK$59,900 for a HK$4 million home** and falls to zero at
**HK$4,323,780**. Above that, nothing changed.

#### Who it reaches

Based on 2024–25 sales, the government estimated the change would cover **about
15% of property transactions**, at a cost of **about HK$400 million a year** in
lost revenue. These are the cheapest homes on the market, mostly small flats,
which is where first-time buyers and young families start.

#### What's happened since

The change has applied to every sale since 26 February 2025. The Inland Revenue
Department's stamp duty statistics will show whether more sales now land just
under HK$4 million than before. That's the first thing we'll check.

#### The case for

**Help at the moment cash is tightest.** A first-time buyer pays the duty on
top of the deposit. Saving up to about HK$60,000 then makes a real difference.

**Cheap and well aimed.** It only touches the bottom of the market, and costs
about HK$400 million a year.

#### The case against

**A cliff invites bunching.** The saving is largest at exactly HK$4 million and
gone by about HK$4.32 million, a strong pull for prices to cluster just under
the line. Where buyers compete for those flats, sellers can take part of the
saving by asking more.

**It helps people who can already buy.** Renters get nothing, including tenants
in the subdivided flats covered in our look back at Basic Housing Units.

#### Our recommendation

1. **Keep the band, but stop moving it by hand.** Link the HK$100 ceiling to a
   home price index, so it keeps pace without waiting for a Budget.
2. **Publish sales counts in HK$100,000 steps around the line**, so anyone can
   see whether prices bunch below HK$4 million and who the saving really
   reaches.

#### What we're watching

- The share of sales between HK$3 million and HK$4.32 million, before and after
  February 2025.
- Whether sale prices cluster just under HK$4 million.
- The real cost in lost revenue against the HK$400 million estimate.
