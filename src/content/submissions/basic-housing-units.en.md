A look back, written in October 2026, at the Basic Housing Unit regime first
announced in the 2024 Policy Address.

#### What changed

**A floor for the worst homes in the city.** The 2024 Policy Address (16 October
2024) said the government would set minimum standards for subdivided flats. The
Basic Housing Units Ordinance took effect on **1 March 2026**. A flat must now
have at least 8 square metres of floor space, a floor-to-ceiling height of at
least 2.3 metres, its own toilet, adequate lighting and ventilation, and
separate water and electricity meters.

**A year to register, three more to fix.** Owners of existing subdivided flats
have until **28 February 2027** to register them. Registration is free, and a
registered flat can stay on the market while its owner does the work, with until
28 February 2030 to have it recognised. From **1 March 2027**, letting a flat
that is neither registered nor recognised is a criminal offence. Flats created
after 1 March 2026 must be recognised before they can be let.

#### Who it reaches

Hong Kong has around **110,000** subdivided flats. In January 2025 the Secretary
for Housing said about 30% were in poor condition, and put the number of
recognised units expected on the market by 2030 at around **80,000**.

That gap is the whole question. Tenants in the roughly 80,000 flats that can be
brought up to standard get a safer home. Tenants in the roughly 30,000 that
can't will have to move. Those are the cheapest and worst flats in the city, so
their tenants are the people with the fewest other options.

#### What's happened since

- By **20 May 2026**, owners had registered nearly **11,000** flats, about 10% of
  the total. Most were in Yau Tsim Mong, Sham Shui Po, Wan Chai, Eastern and
  Kowloon City.
- At that pace, fewer than half of today's flats would be registered by the
  February 2027 deadline.
- In March 2026, surveyors estimated that certifying a unit would cost an owner
  **at least HK$15,000**.

#### The case for

**A minimum that tenants can rely on.** Light, air, a toilet of your own and
room to stand up are a low bar. Writing it into law gives tenants something
enforceable, instead of depending on a landlord's goodwill.

**Time to fix rather than evict.** Because a registered flat can stay on the
market for up to four years while it's improved, most tenants shouldn't lose
their home overnight.

#### The case against

**Fewer cheap homes.** If around 30,000 flats can't meet the standard, the
regime removes the bottom of the rental market, and the people living there are
the least able to pay for anything else.

**The cost goes somewhere.** An owner who spends at least HK$15,000 on
certification, plus any building work, is likely to pass some of it on in rent.

**The risk lands on the tenant.** Letting an unregistered flat is the owner's
offence, but it's the tenant who has to leave.

#### Our recommendation

We support the standards. What we'd add now:

1. **Publish registrations by district every month**, so it's clear well before
   March 2027 where flats are dropping out of the market.
2. **Line up somewhere to go before enforcement starts.** Tenants of flats that
   won't be registered or recognised should get priority for transitional
   housing and Light Public Housing in the same district, before 1 March 2027,
   not after.

#### What we're watching

- The registration count against the 28 February 2027 deadline.
- How many tenants have to move when enforcement starts, and where they go.
- Rents for recognised units compared with the same flats before.
