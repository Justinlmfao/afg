A look back, written in October 2026, at the public healthcare fee reform the
Hospital Authority announced in March 2025.

#### What changed

**Higher fees.** The reform took effect on **1 January 2026**. The fee for an A&E
visit rose from **HK$180 to HK$400**, though patients in the two most urgent
triage categories, critical and emergency, now pay nothing. Many other public
hospital charges went up too.

**Wider protection.** At the same time:

- **Fee waivers** were widened, so more low-income patients pay nothing.
- A **HK$10,000 yearly cap** on public medical fees was introduced, with no
  means test. Once a patient's bills reach it, the rest of the year's fees are
  waived. Patients have to apply, and self-financed items, such as some
  drugs patients buy themselves, don't count.
- The **Samaritan Fund** means test was loosened, so more middle-income
  families can get help with expensive drugs and medical devices.

The Hospital Authority expected the number of people covered by these
protections to rise from about 300,000 to about **1.4 million**.

#### Who it reaches

Four patients, before and after:

- **A low-income patient** is now more likely to have fees waived, and pays
  less or nothing.
- **A middle-income adult with a non-urgent A&E visit** pays HK$400 instead of
  HK$180, HK$220 more each time. They are unlikely to qualify for a waiver or
  reach the cap, so they pay more and gain little.
- **A patient with a chronic illness, just over the waiver limit,** pays more
  for each visit, but the HK$10,000 cap limits the year's total. Whether they
  come out ahead depends on whether their bills pass the cap, and on whether
  they apply for it.
- **A patient with a serious illness who needs new drugs** can now get Samaritan
  Fund help with a looser means test. But self-financed items still sit
  outside the cap.

#### What's happened since

- In the first half of 2026, the 18 public A&E departments recorded **913,248**
  visits, down 3.9% on a year earlier. Non-urgent visits fell by about 20%.
- The share of urgent cases seen within 30 minutes rose from **81.4% to 88.5%**.
- By 30 June 2026, **264,087** fee waivers had been approved, against about
  **14,000** patients given waivers in the year before the reform, nearly 19
  times as many.
- By 30 September 2026, **20,655** patients had been approved for the HK$10,000
  cap.

#### The case for

**Faster care for the urgent cases.** Fewer non-urgent visits have meant
shorter waits for the people who need A&E most.

**Protection reaches far more people.** Nearly 19 times as many patients had
fees waived in the first half of 2026 as in the whole year before.

#### The case against

**The middle pays more.** People who earn too much for a waiver, and don't run
up HK$10,000 a year in bills, carry most of the increase.

**Not every visit that stopped was unnecessary.** Some of the people who stayed
away from A&E may have needed care. The figures so far can't tell the two
apart.

**Protection you have to apply for.** The cap only helps patients who know
about it and apply, which is hardest for the people with the most on their
plate.

#### Our recommendation

1. **Make the HK$10,000 cap automatic.** The Hospital Authority already holds
   every patient's bills. It should apply the cap once they reach HK$10,000,
   instead of waiting for an application.
2. **Publish who stopped coming to A&E**, by age, district and triage category,
   so a drop in non-urgent visits can be checked against delayed care.

#### What we're watching

- How many patients reach HK$10,000 in bills, against how many have applied for
  the cap.
- A&E use by age and district, now that the fee is higher.
- How many middle-income families the looser Samaritan Fund means test
  actually helps.
