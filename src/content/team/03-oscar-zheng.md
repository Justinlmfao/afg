Oscar co-founded AFG because he refused to accept that caring about a problem
and solving it should be two different things. Drawing on the social sciences
and economics, he studies the forces that hold people back. He believes
advocacy is how young people can move those forces, and that one
well-researched voice, raised at the right moment, can shift a decision
affecting thousands.

The problems that interest him most are the ones that look permanent: unequal
access to education, opportunity that follows family income, and communities
that fall behind and stay there. His study of the social sciences has convinced
him that none of these is a law of nature. Each is the result of choices, and
choices can be questioned, argued over and changed by anyone willing to do the
work.

On AFG's research team, Oscar turns that conviction into proposals built on
evidence and aimed at impact. He sees AFG as proof that students don't need
permission to start changing the world. They need the courage to begin and the
rigour to be taken seriously. His ambition is to spend his life on the side of
the argument that widens opportunity, and to show a generation of young people
that their voices belong in the rooms where the future is decided.
